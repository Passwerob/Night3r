Night3R pose-evaluation audit (2026-07-16)
============================================================

Scope
-----

This audit covers the trajectory results produced from the final checkpoint:

    outputs/night3r_mvsec_only_adapt_1k_from_best/last_event_dark3r.pth

It does not change the direct depth metrics (AbsRel, delta_1, RMSE, and related
statistics), because those metrics compare the predicted depth map with depth
GT and do not use point-map registration or trajectory chaining.


1. Confirmed evaluation errors
------------------------------

1.1 Failed point-map registrations were accepted as valid poses

The evaluator registers the same predicted image point map expressed in two
camera coordinate systems using a trimmed Umeyama Sim(3) fit. The old code
returned success whenever enough points survived trimming. It did not reject a
fit with a very large normalized 3D RMSE. Consequently, Night3R registrations
with residuals around 1.0--1.3 were chained into trajectories and reported as
ATE/RPE, even though a single rigid transform did not explain the two point
maps. The resulting 95--156 degree rotation numbers were not valid pose
measurements.

Correction:

    --max-pointmap-residual 0.25

The residual is the trimmed 3D RMSE divided by the median spatial extent of the
target point map. A registration above 0.25 is now rejected with:

    reason = pointmap_residual_above_threshold

Rejected edges are excluded from trajectory chaining and cannot produce
ATE/RPE. The threshold retains the verified MASt3R pairs (roughly 0.02--0.20 in
the pair audit) and rejects the current Night3R pairs (roughly 0.9--1.7 over the
full evaluation).

1.2 Position alignment contaminated the absolute rotation metric

The old trajectory code applied one position-based Umeyama Sim(3) alignment to
the complete camera poses and then computed both translation and rotation
metrics from those transformed poses. On a short, nearly collinear camera path,
the position alignment is underconstrained around the motion axis. This can
produce a large arbitrary alignment roll even when adjacent relative rotations
are accurate. For example, the DL3DV MASt3R 8-frame smoke test had a mean pair
rotation error of 0.60 degrees and rotation RPE of 0.73 degrees, but the old APE
rotation was 151.64 degrees.

Correction:

    translation metrics: per-scene Sim(3) position/scale alignment
    rotation metrics: first-pose origin alignment

After the correction, the same smoke test has APE rotation 2.06 degrees and RPE
rotation 0.73 degrees. This keeps the monocular scale correction needed by ATE
without injecting a position-alignment degeneracy into camera orientation.

1.3 Failed bidirectional diagnostics selected the first direction

When both bidirectional registrations failed, the evaluator stored diagnostics
from the first direction rather than the lower-residual direction. This did not
affect ATE/RPE, because both transforms were already invalid, but it made the
failure residual less informative. The evaluator now selects the lower-residual
diagnostic direction even when both directions are rejected and aggregates a
registration residual over all attempted edges.


2. Coordinate and direction audit
---------------------------------

The relative-pose direction itself is correct.

For the forward pair (image 1, image 2), Night3R/MASt3R predicts:

    pred1[pts3d]                         image-1 points in camera-1 coordinates
    pred2[pts3d_in_other_view]           image-2 points in camera-1 coordinates

For the reversed pair (image 2, image 1), it predicts:

    reverse_pred1[pts3d]                 image-2 points in camera-2 coordinates
    reverse_pred2[pts3d_in_other_view]   image-1 points in camera-2 coordinates

The evaluator therefore registers either image-2 point maps or image-1 point
maps to recover T_camera2_from_camera1. The dataset relative pose uses the same
direction:

    T_camera2_from_camera1 = inverse(T_world_from_camera2)
                             * T_world_from_camera1

DL3DV camera axes are converted from OpenGL to OpenCV with diag(1,-1,-1) on
both sides of the relative transform. Correct MASt3R pair rotations on both
MVSEC and DL3DV confirm that pair order, transform direction, and the DL3DV axis
conversion are not the source of the large old errors.

5. Correct interpretation
-------------------------

The previous Night3R ATE/RPE values must be withdrawn. They do not show that
Night3R has a 95--156 degree pose error; they show that the point-map
registration failed and the evaluator failed to reject it. The valid conclusion
is narrower:

    The final depth-selected Night3R checkpoint cannot currently provide a
    valid rigid camera trajectory under this bidirectional point-map protocol.

No Real-versus-Zero-versus-Shuffle pose claim can be made, because all three
Night3R variants have zero valid edges. Direct depth results remain valid and
can still support the aligned-event ablation on DSEC/MVSEC.


6. Why the final checkpoint has this failure
---------------------------------------------

The final MVSEC-only 1k stage freezes the MASt3R backbone/head, but initializes
from:

    outputs/night3r_oldtest_recovery_from_best_currenttest_train_4gpu_3k/
        last_event_dark3r.pth

That 3k stage in turn initializes from the earlier stage-3 checkpoint:

    outputs/night3r_brg_token_reg_bf16_4gpu_001/
        event_dark3r_epoch0007_stage3_stageepoch0002_step00036386.pth

The earlier run used nonzero decoder/head learning rates during stage 2/3. Its
training configuration also used:

    symmetric_pairs = false
    pose_gt = 0.0

In train_event_dark3r.py, the strong supervised depth and pose branches call:

    supervised_depth_gt_loss(student_out[0], ...)
    pose_gt_loss(student_out[0], ...)

Thus the first-view depth receives strong supervision, while the second-view
cross-view point map is not given the same GT constraint. Feature distillation
and structure consistency touch both outputs, but their weights are much lower
than the first-view depth term. This training recipe can improve direct depth
while allowing the cross-view XYZ/rotation branch to drift. The final 3k and 1k
adapter-only stages inherit that drift; they do not create or repair it.


7. Required changes before claiming pose performance
-----------------------------------------------------

1. Train with symmetric pair order or apply equivalent losses to both outputs.
2. Supervise both same-view depth and cross-view point maps, not only
   student_out[0].
3. Keep the pretrained MASt3R decoder/head frozen until a bidirectional rigid
   consistency loss is active and validated.
4. Add a per-batch bidirectional registration/cycle diagnostic during training.
5. Select pose checkpoints using valid-edge recovery and residual in addition
   to depth AbsRel.
6. Report trajectory coverage together with ATE/RPE; never report ATE/RPE for a
   method whose registrations fail the validity test.

---------------

The pose evaluator test suite contains seven passing tests, including known
PnP recovery, essential-matrix recovery, robust Sim(3) recovery with outliers,
non-rigid point-map rejection, failed-direction diagnostic selection, and the
